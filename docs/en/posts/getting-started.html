Getting started with bujo

Published on November 17, 2026 · start here

bujo.sh provides two Bash functions: bujo_new to create your journal files, and bujo_migrate to carry your tasks from one period to the next. There is nothing to install, nothing to configure: just a plain Bash program and text files, one per period.

1. Load the script

The functions are not binaries: they must be loaded into the current shell with source. For daily use, add this line at the end of your ~/.bashrc: 

# in ~/.bashrc
source ~/path/to/bujo.sh

Then reload your shell, or simply run:

source ~/path/to/bujo.sh
Tip: create a dedicated folder for your journal, say ~/bujo, and always work inside it. Every command creates and looks for files in the current directory.

2. Create your year

A single command is enough to start the year:

bujo_new 2026

This creates a file named 2026 with the header, a task area and the list of the twelve months:

2026
. existing task
---
Jan
Feb
Mar
Apr
May
Jun
Jul
Aug
Sep
Oct
Nov
Dec

Every line matters: the first one is the header, the month names are visual markers, and the two lines . existing task and --- form the task area (see the article on migration for their role).

3. Create your month

At the start of each month, pass its English abbreviation after the year:

bujo_new 2026 Nov

The file 2026-Nov uses the same structure, with every day of the month:

2026 Nov
. existing task
---
1 Sun
2 Mon
3 Tue
…
16 Mon
17 Tue
…
30 Mon

Note the filename: 2026-Nov (year + English month abbreviation), and no leading zero for days below 10.

4. Create your first day

Finally, each morning, create the current day:

bujo_new 2026 Nov 17

The three arguments produce a file named 2026-Nov-17-Tue that includes the weekday in its name and provides hourly slots from 8H to 20H: 

2026 Nov 17 Tue
. existing task
---
8H
9H
10H
…
19H
20H
Hint: count the day rows in 2026-Nov to find the right day number, or just keep creating files in the same folder — the month keeps them all together anyway.

Check that everything works

A quick test:

$ source bujo.sh
$ bujo_new 2026
created 2026
$ bujo_new 2026 Nov
created 2026-Nov
$ bujo_new 2026 Nov 17
created 2026-Nov-17-Tue
$ ls
2026  2026-Nov  2026-Nov-17-Tue

Your three files sit side by side. You are ready to fill in your first day — head over to Using bujo on a day-to-day basis.
