Using bujo on a day-to-day basis

Published on November 17, 2026 · practice

Once your files are created, the logbook is kept day after day: in the morning you open the current day, write your tasks with simple markers, and log what happened in the hourly slots. In the evening, one call to bujo_migrate puts everything back in its place.

The morning ritual

  1. Create the day if not done yet: bujo_new 2026 Nov 17.
  2. Open the file 2026-Nov-17-Tue in your favorite editor.
  3. List what you want to achieve.

A first filled-in file looks like this:

2026 Nov 17 Tue
> send the quarterly expense report
< propose a monthly meeting to the team
x prepare the sprint review
~ buy a new keyboard
. task in progress today
@MIGRATED@
8H Breakfast and planning
9H
10H Team stand-up
11H
12H Lunch
13H
14H Client meeting
15H
16H
17H
18H
19H
20H

The task markers

In the task area, every line starts with a single prefix that decides what happens to the task during migration. The whole spirit of bujo lies in these five characters:

  • . task — the task stays here: it is not moved during migration. It is also the prefix a file receives when a task arrives from a finer period (from a day into its month, for example).
  • > task — the task follows the period: a “>” in a day goes to the next day, a “>” in a month to the next month, a “>” in a year to the next year.
  • < task — the task moves up a level: a “<” in a day moves up to its month, a “<” in a month moves up to its year.
  • x task — a completed version of a task already present at the upper level. During migration it marks the original as done, without rewriting it.
  • ~ task — a deferred version of an upper-level task: same principle as x, but to postpone it instead.
Remember: x and ~ only apply to tasks that already exist at the upper level. If there is no match, they are simply ignored during migration — never added.

Filling in the day

The 8H to 20H lines act as an hourly log: put a short note after each slot, or leave empty what has not happened yet. You can also use them as mere time anchors in a free-flowing day.

8H — 9H 9H — 10H 10H — 11H 11H — 12H 12H — 13H 13H — 14H 14H — 15H 15H — 16H 16H — 17H 17H — 18H 18H — 19H 19H — 20H

What about tasks that actually progress?

Keep it simple: add a line, remove a line, change the prefix. A task started as > write the budget and nearly finished becomes x write the budget (the “>” version will still show up in the following day, since it was part of today's log — we cover this in the article on migration).

A typical day, in short

  1. Morning: bujo_new 2026 Nov 17 then fill in the tasks of the day.
  2. During the day: maintain the task area and annotate the hourly slots.
  3. Evening: mark finished tasks with x, deferred ones with ~, to carry over with >, to move up with <, then run bujo_migrate 2026-Nov-17-Tue.

The evening command is the heart of the system — it files your tasks away for tomorrow. Let's look at it in detail in the article on migration.
