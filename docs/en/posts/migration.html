Migration: closing your day, your month, your year

Published on November 17, 2026 · migration

The nightly habit — ending your day with bujo_migrate — turns your logbook into a self-filing system: unfinished tasks carry over to tomorrow, the ones that must move up climb into the month, and the month's into the year. Everything goes through a single command: 

bujo_migrate <file>

The three forms of migration

The shape of the filename determines how entries are redistributed:

  • Day (2026-Nov-17-Tue) — lines starting with > go to the next day, lines starting with < move up to the month 2026-Nov, and x/~ update the month's tasks.
  • Month (2026-Nov) — lines starting with > go to the next month (2026-Dec), lines starting with < move up to the year 2026, and x/~ update the year's tasks.
  • Year (2026) — lines starting with > go to the next year (2027). No upper level here.
Golden rule: if the destination does not exist yet, it is simply skipped. You must therefore create the next day in advance (bujo_migrate 2026-Nov-17-Tue only affects the next day if 2026-Nov-18-Wed exists). Create your files ahead of time: bujo_new 2026 Nov 18 as early as the 17th, bujo_new 2026 Dec at the end of November, and bujo_new 2027 before New Year's Day.

What the migration does, step by step

Take the November 17 file filled as in the previous article:

2026 Nov 17 Tue
> send the quarterly expense report
< propose a monthly meeting to the team
x prepare the sprint review
~ buy a new keyboard
. task in progress today
---
8H Breakfast and planning
…

In the evening, run:

bujo_migrate 2026-Nov-17-Tue

And the next day's and month's files are updated:

# 2026-Nov-18-Wed (excerpt)
2026 Nov 18 Wed
. existing task
. send the quarterly expense report
---
8H
# 2026-Nov (excerpt)
2026 Nov
. propose a monthly meeting to the team
x prepare the sprint review
---
1 Sun

What just happened?

  • > send the quarterly expense report → arrived in the next day, prefix converted to .: it awaits your hand tomorrow.
  • < propose a monthly meeting to the team → moved up to the month as . propose…. Since the month already contained . propose…, the line was not duplicated.
  • x prepare the sprint review, already present in the month as . prepare… → its prefix changed to x: the monthly task is marked as done.
  • ~ buy a new keyboard has no match in the month → it is ignored, without creating a duplicate.
  • The leading . does not migrate: it stays in the day's file.

The role of the --- marker

Every file — year, month or day — contains a line --- that acts as an insertion point. Entries arriving in a file are placed right before the marker, where the active tasks live. The marker stays in place after each migration, ready for the next one. It is what separates the “task area” (above it) from the rest of the file (a month's days, a day's hourly slots).

Important: if a destination file has no --- marker, the migration stops with an error. Never remove this line from your files.

The monthly and yearly loop

  • End of month: bujo_migrate 2026-Nov carries the > lines into 2026-Dec, moves the < lines up into 2026, and records the state of the yearly tasks.
  • End of year: bujo_migrate 2026 carries the > lines into 2027 — the time to create that year with bujo_new 2027.

A month can only migrate if the next month exists, and a year only if the next year exists. The rule never changes: create first, migrate second.
